title: "Dedicated Synchronizer Traffic"
description: "Pay for traffic on a registered dedicated synchronizer with Canton Coin burned on the Global Synchronizer"

A dedicated synchronizer can charge for traffic the way the Global Synchronizer does. Its members buy traffic for it with Canton Coin (CC) on the Global Synchronizer, and the synchronizer's operator grants that traffic on its own sequencer. This page explains how that works and how a validator buys traffic for a dedicated synchronizer. To run such a synchronizer, see [Dedicated Synchronizer Helm Deployment](/global-synchronizer/extension-synchronizers/dedicated-synchronizer-deployment).

## How it works

1. **Registration.** The Super Validators (SVs) register the dedicated synchronizer by a governance vote. The vote creates a `RegisteredSynchronizer` contract on the Global Synchronizer that binds the synchronizer ID to the operator's party and sets a traffic discount.
2. **Purchase.** A validator buys traffic for its participant on the dedicated synchronizer. The purchase is a Global Synchronizer transaction that burns CC like any other [traffic purchase](/global-synchronizer/deployment/synchronizer-traffic), with the registration attached. The `MemberTraffic` contract that records it names the operator as an observer.
3. **Grant.** The operator's sync operator app sees the purchase and raises the participant's traffic limit on the dedicated synchronizer's sequencer to the participant's total purchased traffic.
4. **Use.** The participant submits transactions on the dedicated synchronizer, and the sequencer deducts traffic from that limit.

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant V as Validator
    participant GS as Global Synchronizer
    participant SO as Sync operator app
    participant DS as Dedicated synchronizer

    V->>GS: Buy traffic for the dedicated synchronizer (burns CC)
    GS-->>SO: Purchase recorded, operator is an observer
    SO->>DS: Set the traffic limit to the purchased total
    V->>DS: Submit transactions, drawing down the limit
```

A purchase for a synchronizer that is not registered is refused.

## Price

Traffic on a registered dedicated synchronizer costs the Global Synchronizer's price, `extraTrafficPrice` in the [traffic parameters](/global-synchronizer/deployment/synchronizer-traffic#traffic-parameters), times the registration's discount factor. The factor is greater than 0 and at most 1, where 1 is the full price. For example, at 60 USD per MB and a factor of 0.5, 60 USD of CC buys 2 MB.

Every purchase must be at least the Global Synchronizer's `minTopupAmount`. The SVs set the discount factor when they register the synchronizer and can change it by a later vote.

## Traffic parameters

The operator's sync operator app keeps these traffic parameters on the dedicated synchronizer:

- **No free traffic.** The base traffic amount is zero, so a participant can only send what it has bought. That includes topology transactions such as party allocations and package vetting.
- **The Global Synchronizer's read cost.** The read versus write scaling factor follows the Global Synchronizer's `readVsWriteScalingFactor`.
- **Free confirmation responses.** Confirmation responses are not charged.

The operator's mediators have unlimited traffic, since they cannot buy any. Otherwise, [traffic accounting](/global-synchronizer/deployment/synchronizer-traffic#traffic-accounting-what-counts-as-traffic) works as on the Global Synchronizer.

## Join a dedicated synchronizer

The operator admits only participants it has permissioned, and a new member has no traffic on the synchronizer. Connecting already costs traffic, because the participant sends its parties and package vetting to the synchronizer when it connects. So a validator joins in this order, running the commands in its participant's [Canton console](/global-synchronizer/reference/canton-console-reference#participant-console):

1. **Ask the operator for access.** Send your participant ID, which `participant.id.toProtoPrimitive` prints. The operator permissions it and sends you the URL of the synchronizer's sequencer and the synchronizer ID.
2. **Register the connection.** Register the synchronizer with a handshake but do not connect yet. The handshake makes your participant a member of the synchronizer and costs no traffic. `manualConnect = true` keeps a restart from connecting before you have traffic:

   ```scala
   participant.synchronizers.register_by_config(
     SynchronizerConnectionConfig.tryGrpcSingleConnection(
       "dedicated",
       SequencerAlias.Default,
       "https://sequencer.dedicated.example.com",
       manualConnect = true,
     )
   )
   ```

3. **Buy traffic** for your participant on the synchronizer, as described in [Buy traffic manually](#buy-traffic-manually). Buy only after step 2: the operator's app grants a purchase only to a participant the synchronizer already knows, and a purchase made earlier waits for your next one. Once the request's [status](/reference/splice-wallet-api-external/post-v0walletbuy-traffic-requests:tracking_idstatus) is completed, ask the operator to confirm the grant. They see it on their sequencer; your participant only shows its traffic once it is connected.
4. **Connect:**

   ```scala
   participant.synchronizers.reconnect("dedicated")
   ```

Then add the synchronizer to your validator's configuration, with the same alias, as shown in [Automatic top-ups](#automatic-top-ups), so the validator stays connected and tops up by itself. Add it only after this first grant: the validator app connects to every synchronizer in `extraDomains` as part of starting up, and connecting needs traffic.

## Buy traffic

### Automatic top-ups

The validator app's [automatic top-ups](/global-synchronizer/deployment/validator-kubernetes#configuring-automatic-traffic-purchases) work for dedicated synchronizers too, with the same `targetThroughput` and `minTopupInterval` settings, set per synchronizer. They only run while the participant is connected to the synchronizer, which is why the first purchase is manual. The validator reads the synchronizer's registration from Scan for each top-up and skips the top-up while Scan has none. The CC comes from the validator's wallet, as for the Global Synchronizer.

With the `splice-validator` Helm chart, set them on the synchronizer's entry in `extraDomains`:

```yaml
extraDomains:
  - alias: "dedicated"
    url: "https://sequencer.dedicated.example.com"
    topup:
      targetThroughput: 20000
      minTopupInterval: "1m"
```

Otherwise, set the same in the validator app's configuration, for example through an [`ADDITIONAL_CONFIG` environment variable](/global-synchronizer/deployment/configuration#adding-ad-hoc-configuration):

```
canton.validator-apps.validator_backend.domains.extra = [{
  alias = "dedicated"
  url = "https://sequencer.dedicated.example.com"
  topup {
    target-throughput = 20000
    min-topup-interval = 1m
  }
}]
```

The validator app applies this `url` to the participant's connection each time it starts. Two consequences:

- After the operator [upgrades the synchronizer](/global-synchronizer/extension-synchronizers/dedicated-synchronizer-upgrades), your participant moves to the new sequencer by itself, but change `url` to the new URL the operator sends you, or the next restart points it back at the old one.
- An entry holds one sequencer URL. On a synchronizer that runs several sequencers, it replaces a connection to several of them with a connection to one.

### Buy traffic manually

To buy traffic once, send a [buy traffic request](/reference/splice-wallet-api-external/post-v0walletbuy-traffic-requests) to the wallet API with `domain_id` set to the dedicated synchronizer's ID and `receiving_validator_party_id` set to a party hosted on the participant that should receive the traffic.

To check that a synchronizer is registered, look up its registration on Scan at `GET /api/scan/v0/synchronizers/{synchronizer_id}/registration`, or through your validator's scan proxy at `GET /api/validator/v0/scan-proxy/synchronizers/{synchronizer_id}/registration`. Both return 404 for a synchronizer that is not registered.

### Check your traffic

Once connected, read your participant's traffic on the dedicated synchronizer in its console:

```scala
participant.traffic_control.traffic_state(participant.synchronizers.id_of("dedicated"))
```

Scan's member traffic status endpoint serves the Global Synchronizer only and rejects a dedicated synchronizer's ID.

## Trust

The dedicated synchronizer's sequencer applies whatever traffic limit the operator sets. Nothing checks those limits against what members bought, so members rely on the operator to grant the traffic they paid for.
